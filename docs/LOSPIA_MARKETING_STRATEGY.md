# Lospia Marketing Strategy

## B2B SaaS Go-to-Market, Positioning and Landing Page Strategy

**Product:** Lospia
**Stage:** Early production / pilot-ready
**Current live app:** `https://lospia.vercel.app`
**Primary pilot workspace:** AF Operasyon
**Primary pilot customer context:** Aslı Filinta operations
**Document purpose:** This document defines how Lospia should be positioned, marketed, structured and presented before building the public marketing website.

---

## 1. Strategic Positioning Summary

Lospia is a premium, simple and structured operations panel for small and mid-sized brand, fashion, textile, e-commerce, creative studio and operations teams.

These teams often manage their daily work through:

* scattered Excel sheets
* WhatsApp conversations
* manual follow-ups
* verbal approvals
* informal rules
* memory-based management

This creates operational confusion:

* tasks get lost
* approvals are forgotten
* deadlines are missed
* ownership is unclear
* managers constantly ask for updates
* weekly progress is hard to see
* operations depend too much on memory

Lospia’s strategic position is to turn this scattered, informal work style into a clear, premium operations panel.

Lospia is **not**:

* a generic ClickUp, Monday, Asana or Trello clone
* a heavy enterprise PLM system
* an engineering/Jira-style workflow tool
* a blank-canvas Notion/Airtable replacement
* an AI hype product

Lospia is:

* a simple operations system for brand teams
* a bridge from Excel and WhatsApp to structured workflows
* an approval and visibility layer for managers
* a lightweight alternative between generic task tools and heavy PLM systems
* a productized service plus SaaS platform

---

## 2. Core Product Narrative

### Before Lospia

A brand team tracks production, campaigns, design approvals and operations through Excel sheets and WhatsApp messages.

Typical questions are unclear:

* Was this fabric approved?
* Who owns this task?
* When is the lookbook due?
* Who is waiting for Aslı Hanım?
* Which tasks are delayed this week?
* Which work is blocked?
* What changed since last week?

The workflow feels practical at first, but becomes fragile as the team grows.

The result is mental load, repeated follow-ups, missed approvals and unclear accountability.

### After Lospia

Operations move into a clear panel.

Every task has:

* an owner
* a responsible person
* collaborators
* a due date
* a status
* a category
* approval visibility
* rules and standards

Managers can quickly see:

* what is being worked on
* what is overdue
* who is waiting for approval
* which work is completed
* what needs attention this week

The main transformation:

> From scattered Excel and WhatsApp follow-up to a clear, structured operations system.

Lospia does not only sell software. It sells clarity, control and peace of mind.

## 4. Market Gap

The market has two extremes.

### Generic project management tools

Examples:

* Monday.com
* ClickUp
* Asana
* Trello
* Notion
* Airtable

Strengths:

* flexible
* broad use cases
* many integrations
* many templates

Weaknesses for Lospia’s target customer:

* too generic
* too many features
* too much setup
* blank-canvas problem
* not designed for approvals and operational routines
* feels like another software project to configure

### Heavy fashion / PLM / enterprise systems

Examples:

* Centric PLM
* Infor
* WFX
* Zedonk
* BlueCherry

Strengths:

* deep fashion/product lifecycle features
* supplier and production tools
* enterprise-grade workflows

Weaknesses for Lospia’s target customer:

* expensive
* slow to implement
* too heavy for small teams
* requires training and process transformation
* not suitable for lightweight operational visibility

### Lospia’s opportunity

Lospia sits between these two extremes:

> More structured than Excel, WhatsApp and Notion.
> Simpler and more accessible than enterprise PLM.
> More opinionated and brand-operations-focused than ClickUp or Monday.

This is the strategic opening.

## 6. Website Strategy

The public Lospia website should not feel like a generic SaaS template.

It should feel:

* premium
* clear
* operational
* calm
* trustworthy
* simple
* refined
* founder-friendly

The website should sell the transformation, not the feature list.

Main transformation:

> From operational chaos to weekly clarity.

The landing page should follow an inverted pyramid structure:

1. strongest value proposition
2. problem clarity
3. product solution
4. feature proof
5. use cases
6. pilot/social proof
7. pricing direction
8. FAQ
9. final CTA

## 12. Pricing Strategy

Lospia should not start with a pure self-serve Stripe checkout model.

The early-stage model should be:

> Productized service setup + monthly SaaS subscription

### Recommended early model

#### Setup / onboarding fee

One-time setup fee for:

* Excel cleanup
* data import
* workspace setup
* first rules setup
* user onboarding
* initial workflow configuration

Possible range:

* local market: equivalent of a meaningful consulting/setup fee
* international early range: `$250–$500`

#### Monthly workspace subscription

Avoid per-seat pricing at first.

Use workspace-based pricing.

Possible early range:

* small team workspace: `$99–$149 / month`
* includes up to a limited number of users
* extra users later if needed

### Why not Stripe immediately?

Because the target customer likely needs help moving from Excel and WhatsApp.

A blank workspace may lead to churn.

Early sales should be high-touch:

* demo call
* Excel review
* setup
* pilot
* weekly check-in
* conversion

### Pricing page direction

Do not show a complex pricing table yet.

Use a “pilot / setup call” model:

* `Kurulum destekli pilot`
* `Excel aktarımı dahil`
* `Ekibinize göre fiyatlandırma`
* `Demo görüşmesi planlayın`

## 18. What Not To Build Yet

Do not build these yet:

### Stripe self-serve checkout

Reason:

* too early
* onboarding is not fully self-serve
* customers need setup support
* founder-led sales is more valuable now

### Complex AI features

Reason:

* the main pain is operational clarity, not content generation
* AI hype may dilute positioning
* approval/task visibility matters more

### Native mobile apps

Reason:

* responsive web is enough
* native apps add maintenance cost
* mobile-first web/PWA can come later

### Complex integrations

Avoid for now:

* Zapier
* Slack
* Gmail OAuth
* ERP integrations
* PLM integrations
* warehouse systems

Focus on:

* Excel import
* approvals
* roles
* rules
* weekly visibility

### Too many views

Avoid becoming ClickUp.

Keep the product opinionated and simple.
